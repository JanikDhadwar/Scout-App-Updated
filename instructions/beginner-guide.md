# Beginner scouting guide

Scouting means recording what robots can do and what you actually see them do. Your team uses that evidence to prepare for matches and choose alliance partners.

## Start with observations

Pit scouting is an interview about a robot's design and reported capabilities. Match scouting records actions and outcomes during play. A team's claim that it can climb is different from observing a successful climb.

## Record unknowns honestly

Zero means you observed none. Not observed means you could not see or do not know. Not attempted means the robot never tried. A failure means you saw an unsuccessful attempt. These answers lead to different conclusions.

## Your first event

Owners and admins choose an event, download its schedule, copy optional starter forms, add season-specific questions, map measurements, and approve what members may see. Scouts open their assigned teams and use the same forms throughout the event. Temporary handoffs cover one match without changing permanent ownership.

## Before submitting

Quick guidance offers question help on demand. Guided forms show hints and examples. The final review always follows the scouting questions. Check missing answers and warnings, then submit. Suggestions do not block submission, but required fields and valid answers still apply.

## Learn by doing

Open Practice for illustrated scenarios with known answers and private explanations. Try a missed autonomous attempt, a blocked view, and an endgame non-attempt. Free practice with workspace forms remains available. Practice never changes competition statistics or real assignments.

## Make a decision

Review source reports, sample sizes, missing observations, and disagreements before trusting an insight. Match intelligence prepares the drive team for opponents. Present All supports alliance selection with a candidate summary for every event team.
